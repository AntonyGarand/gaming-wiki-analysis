The experiment01

A very silly game, taken seriously

Start at Falador, the white city. Its first link is Capital, because Falador is one. Capital's first link is Kingdom. Kingdom's is Gielinor, the world the game runs on. Gielinor's is Guthix, the god who shaped it. Guthix's is Gods, and Gods points back at Gielinor. Five clicks from a castle to a circle. You can keep clicking forever; the circle doesn't care.

People noticed years ago that on Wikipedia this game nearly always ends the same way, in the loop that runs through Philosophy. It caught on because it feels like it should mean something: click enough first links and you learn what an encyclopedia thinks everything is made of. We wanted the gaming version of that answer, so we fetched wikis and followed every first link of every article.

The rules02

The rules, such as they are

A wiki offers a hundred ways to cheat at this game. Infoboxes and navigation templates are packed with links, but nobody reads those as sentences, so the walk ignores them. Links inside parentheses are usually disambiguators, like Dragon (race); italics mark titles and foreign words. Skipped, skipped. Links that land on a disambiguation page or a date page, which on a game wiki is an update log wearing an article costume, are skipped too. Red links, images, categories: skipped. If the first link fails a test, the walk takes the next one. If nothing on the page passes, the walk ends and the article goes in the graveyard.

What survives the skipping is the part of the wiki that explains itself in sentences, the chain of read-this-first that a real reader falls down. The panel is running one now, on a random article. Every pill is one click. The walks come out shorter than you'd think: the median article on this wiki is in its loop after clicks, and the longest walk in the whole wiki takes .

Your turn10

Feed it an article

Type anything and watch it walk: a boss, a quest, an item, a city. Guess which whirlpool it falls into before the pills stop. You get scarily good at this. Then switch the rank and follow the same article's fifth link instead. It usually ends somewhere completely different, occasionally somewhere better.

Caveats, for honesty's sake. Everything here is one snapshot of wikis that change every day. The parser trips over the occasional exotic template, and when it does, that page's walk is wrong; we believe this is rare, and we would say that either way. The rules, skip the parentheses, skip the italics, are a house style, not a law of nature. The random rank is one seeded draw per article, so read its numbers as about right rather than exact.
